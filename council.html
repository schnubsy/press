A Private Collective · Capabilities Prospectus

Twenty-one minds.
In service of one.

The Council is a standing collective of elite specialists — designers, advisors, and operators — convened on demand, built to disagree well, and sworn to a single outcome: the best possible version of your decision, your design, and your build.

Ethos

Built on productive disagreement

Most advice fails in one of two ways: a single voice with a single bias, or a crowd that politely agrees. The Council is engineered against both — a fixed cast of twenty-one named specialists under three heads, each carrying a strong home philosophy, a declared bias, and the professional discipline to serve the work anyway.

Built to disagree

Every seat's conviction is another seat's counterweight — each member's "optimizes for" is deliberately another's "will sacrifice." Consensus is earned in open debate, never assumed and never polite.

Specialists, unleashed

Each member does the one thing they are world-class at, and nothing else. The structure exists so no one has to be mediocre at everything — and so a declared bias can be corrected for.

Finished work

Debates end in verdicts. Reviews end in assets. Orders end in delivery. The Principal receives the thing itself and the reasoning that survived — never a status update in its place.

Bias shapes how, never whether

Each specialist is opinionated by design but professional by oath: their philosophy shapes how they solve your problem, never whether they serve it.

Steelman before critique

No idea is attacked before its strongest honest version has been stated. Strawmen are beneath this Council.

Memory with a firewall

The collective keeps a private dossier of how you think — read silently by the leads to sharpen judgment, never shown to the working cast, so their output stays honest.

Provenance

How the Council was assembled

None of these seats is arbitrary. Each head was recruited against an explicit theory of cognitive diversity — the goal was never a team that gets along, but a team whose disagreements cover the whole territory of a problem.

The Ear — de Bono's hats, then beyond them

The counsel bench was recruited around Edward de Bono's Six Thinking Hats — the classic proof that a group reasons best when its members are assigned opposing modes rather than left to converge. Six seats map to the hats directly: Proof wears White (facts and data), Soul wears Red (feeling and intuition), Champion wears Yellow (optimism and value), Sentinel wears Black (caution and risk), Maker wears Green (creativity and alternatives), and Alistair wears Blue — the process hat, refereeing the rest.

Then the bench was extended, because the hats cover modes of thinking but not sources of knowledge: Premise interrogates the question itself before any hat goes on; Owl brings what the field already knows so the collective never reasons from scratch; Pulse brings the one voice de Bono's room lacks entirely — the market's.

The Eye — a spectrum of opposed schools

The design cast was recruited in deliberate tension pairs from across the schools: rationalist structure (Grid) against emotional image-making (Vogue); brutal machine honesty (Steel) against calm human-centeredness (Calm); futurist spectacle (Neon) against platform tradition (Cupertino). One seat sits outside every school and owns whether any of it works (Flow), and the chair went to the tradition that refuses to separate function, craft, and the human at all — Danish Modern, in the person of Marlowe, who judges without authoring.

The Hand — the four verbs of delivery

Execution decomposes into exactly four verbs, and each got a seat: Forge builds, Quill writes, Steward runs, Snag proves — under a career chief of staff (Garrick) whose only craft is turning a decision into the finished thing.

The common thread: a fixed cast with declared biases. Rotation would make each session smarter and the collective dumber — familiarity is what lets you calibrate a voice, and a declared bias is the only kind you can correct for.

First Head · Design

The Eye

Design here is judged, not voted on. Six authors from deliberately opposed schools each argue a single direction; a seventh seat, the interaction architect, audits whether any of it actually works; and one director weighs them all, scores, and decides. No direction wins by comfort or committee — it wins by surviving cross-examination against five rivals and a UX pass that shows no mercy. Beauty is the ambition; function is the floor. Nothing beautiful-but-broken has ever left this room.

What the Eye delivers

  • FULL review — six genuinely divergent directions, each anchored to a named design movement (no two share one), rendered as live interactive mock UI across every primary flow, scored on a project-weighted rubric, with a winner, runner-up, and optional hybrid.
  • Build-ready handoff — after you confirm the winner: compilable design tokens (Swift or CSS, stack-detected), exhaustive design-system specs for the top two, a paste-ready design-system prompt for any AI coding tool, and RECOMMENDATION.md.
  • LITE ideation — 3–4 fast hero-screen mockups to feel out territory before committing. No scoring, no assets, genuinely quick.
  • The Flow Review — every direction, full or lite, is independently audited for UX soundness — flows, states, error paths, accessibility — before it reaches you. Nothing beautiful-but-broken ships.
  • Physical space — rooms, structures, environments. The Eye's remit covers built space, not just screens.
How it runs: Marlowe orients on your actual project, holds one short intake meeting for your steer, briefs the cast (dossier firewalled), the six author, Flow audits, Marlowe scores and recommends — then pauses exactly once for your winner pick before cutting handoff assets. Say "quick ideas" for LITE; anything else runs FULL.

Second Head · Counsel

The Ear

Counsel here is adversarial by charter. Before a single objection lands, the strongest honest version of your idea is stated aloud — no strawman survives this room. Then eight specialists, each assigned an opposing mode of thought, try in earnest to break what remains, cross-examining one another as hard as they question you. The Chief referees the clashes, ends them one exchange before they sour, and returns a distilled verdict — the points that matter, where the bench split, and the thing you are avoiding. A verdict, never a transcript.

What the Ear delivers

  • GUT-CHECK — the Chief plus the two or three most relevant seats, a fast sharp take, a 2–4 sentence verdict. The recommended default for quick asks: "is this stupid?"
  • FULL COUNCIL — all eight: steelman first, then individual challenges, then genuine cross-examination refereed by the Chief — ending in an executive summary, never a transcript.
  • CRITIQUE — full cast, evaluative frame: does this built thing or formed idea clear the bar? What's missing? What would a user, reader, or critic say?
  • The distillation — every session ends with the verdict, the 3–5 points that matter ranked, where the council split, the load-bearing critique for how you think — and the thing you're avoiding.
House rules: steelman before critique — no strawmen. Advisors cross-examine each other, not just you, and concede points they lose. Each seat's pre-mortems, evidence demands, and reframes adapt to the domain — a theology paper gets a doctrinal pre-mortem, a life decision a regret pre-mortem.

Third Head · Execution

The Hand

The Hand deals only in finished things. A decision goes in; a work order, a deck, a runbook, or a repaired system comes out — never a status update standing in for the work. Sir Garrick Vale receives every decree, decomposes it, names in one line the seat that will carry it, and holds the whole arc to its close. Forge builds, Quill writes, Steward runs, Snag proves. What leaves this room is meant to be used — not reviewed and handed back for another pass.

What the Hand delivers

  • Forge — Claude Code work orders, technical specs, tech-stack selection, data models, system and functional diagrams. Software architecture lives here — not with the Eye.
  • Quill — PM and stakeholder deliverables: status reports, decks, memos, comms — decision-first, audience-calibrated.
  • Steward — environment maintenance runbooks, config discipline, migration prep, the handoff system, MANUAL.md — and this guide's sync.
  • Snag — QA and testing: full test runs, gap analysis, regression checks, and data-quality validation, every defect severity-flagged with reproduction steps. With sentry, half of the ship-gate.
  • Kickoff routing — a brand-new software project never goes straight to Forge: Garrick routes it to the Kickoff rite first, so the worth-building check and batched interview happen before any scaffold.
What the Hand is not: a debating chamber. If your petition needs vetting, Garrick routes it to the Ear first; if it needs designing, to the Eye — then the Hand builds what survived.

Owner: Quill · trigger — "press this" / "send to the press" / "publish <file>"

Press

Publishes to the open web at schnubsy.github.io/press/<name>.html — the portal auto-discovers, no index edit — in one of two lanes. Lane A — single page: one self-contained HTML file (inline CSS/JS, no build step, no state) pushed with a single GitHub MCP create_or_update_file call to schnubsy/press on main, with the sha omitted for new files so an existing file fails safely instead of overwriting. Reliable below ~100 KB per file. Lane B — publication: a main page plus linked subpages and/or image assets, routed as a whole through a single Claude Code work order (pull, add files, commit, push), because the single-call MCP path is unreliable above ~100 KB and wasteful for asset batches.

Shared rules across both lanes: confirm it's a page, not an app; if the source is private, redact every file — no local paths, dossier locations, hardware, or environment-manual references, subpages and assets included. Filenames are one to three words, lowercase and hyphen-delimited (e.g. divorce-prep-checklist.html); pages sit at the repo root (<name>.html), assets in a root-level <name>-assets/ directory, and subpages link relatively — the portal auto-discovers root-level pages only, so every subpage must be reachable from the main page. Verify byte-exact per file: local git hash-object must equal the blob SHA GitHub returns (never cross-compared against shasum -a 1 — different schemes); Lane B verifies the main page's hash exactly, then the asset count plus one sampled asset hash. Recovery: a wedged Lane A MCP means a full Claude Desktop quit and relaunch, then push — never reroute to a relay; Lane B failures self-resolve inside the Code work order.

Display titles decouple a card's name from its filename. Each page is self-describing — its human title lives in the page's own <title> and a <meta name="page-title">, so the title travels with the file — and a lightweight root manifest, pages.json ({"<file>.html":{"title":"…"}}), lets the portal render every card title in a single fetch, falling back to a titleized filename (hyphens → spaces, title-cased) when a page is absent from the manifest. On publish the action prompts for the display title, offering the titleized filename as the default, then writes both the page (with <title> and <meta> set) and the pages.json entry — failing safe, since the titleize fallback still renders the card if the manifest write is missed.

The archive action retires a page off the portal without deleting it. archive <page> (bare slug) physically moves <page>.html and its <page>-assets/ folder from the repo root into archive/, and moves its manifest entry from root pages.json into archive/pages.json — all in one commit, so relative asset links stay intact (root-absolute self-references are rewritten first, or the move aborts and flags). Because discovery is root-scoped, the page drops off the main portal automatically and surfaces in the archive view — the same auto-discovery logic pointed at archive/, reading archive/pages.json for titles. It runs as Lane B (a page + asset-folder + manifest move is a multi-file git change with no single-call MCP equivalent) and is fully reversible via unarchive.

Owner: Steward · trigger — "sync the council" / "run the audit" / monthly cadence

Steward Audit

The one command that keeps the plugin aligned and propagated across every surface — detect → repair → propagate, replacing the old multi-chat scramble. Detect: diffs plugin.json against the four version-stamp locations (guide nav badge + footer, and the Version: headers in PERSONAS.md and CAPABILITIES.md), flags annex entries newer than the guide's stamp, checks skills against the cast (PERSONAS.md) and against the instrument index (every registered instrument needs its one-line entry in skills/hand/SKILL.md), catches capabilities used but never registered, and detects a stale bundle — a dist/kings-council.plugin older than the newest committed source means Cowork + web/mobile are out of date, whether MANUAL.md (the separate environment repo) trails the plugin's version, and whether the published Press copy of this guide (schnubsy/press/council.html) has fallen behind the generated version — a stale Press copy is flagged as drift, so a missed publish is caught next run. Repair (automatable): fixes all four stamps to match plugin.json and regenerates this guide from its sources. Propagate (emit, don't execute): ready-to-paste Claude Code prompts to commit + push and to rebuild the bundle, plus a manual checklist for the two steps no tool can do — reloading dist/kings-council.plugin in Desktop (the single action that refreshes Cowork and claude.ai web/mobile together) and a fresh-chat cast check to verify. It separates what it fixed itself from what waits on your manual reload. When a run regenerates this guide, propagate also republishes it to the public Press copy (council.html) — gated on the guide having actually changed, lane-aware by size, and hash-verified — so the published prospectus never lags the generated one. Cadence: monthly (co-timed with the log-hygiene job), not only symptom-driven — the monthly run drops an audit-due marker that a SessionStart hook surfaces once per day until you run the audit, which clears it on completion. Owner: Snag · trigger — "run the gauntlet" / "test this" / "QA this" / "find the gaps"

Gauntlet

The Council's quality gate — full test runs, solution review, gap analysis, regression checks, and general QC on anything the Hand produces, plus data-quality validation: schema checks, migration integrity, and whether the numbers survived the pipeline. It inventories what "done" claims to be, runs the project's suite end-to-end (writing targeted tests where none exist), reviews the solution against spec for gaps, edge cases, error paths, and regressions, then reports every defect with a severity flag (🔴 P0 / 🟡 watch / 🟢 fine), reproduction steps, and a verified-vs-inferred label — a clean run is an explicit green report, never silence. Sentry

Ceremony

The two rites

Two ceremonies bracket every project's life: Kickoff opens it, and Handoff closes each arc of work. Between them, the Council's memory system keeps every surface oriented.

Kickoff — idea to handoff-ready repo

Trigger with "new project," "kickoff," "I have an idea for an app/tool." Runs anywhere; scaffolds only in Claude Code.

  1. Worth-building check — one honest pass: names tools that already solve it, offers an Ear gut-check if the verdict is murky.
  2. One batched interview — purpose, users, platform, stack, data sensitivity, name, git remote, v0.1 success. Every question up front; zero drip-feeding after.
  3. Scaffold (Claude Code only) — repo under ~/Documents/VSCode/<name>, git initialized, control files seeded with the real answers.
  4. First arc-close — runs /kings-council:handoff, emits a paste-ready Claude Desktop project block, registers the project in MANUAL.md.
On any other surface the rite returns a single deterministic Claude Code work order instead — nothing half-scaffolded, ever.

Handoff — closing an arc

The command: /kings-council:handoff (Claude Code and Cowork).

  1. Consumes ORCHESTRATION.md — Desktop's append-only inbox of decisions, work-order summaries, and flagged RULE: entries.
  2. Updates CLAUDE.md (durable truth), HANDOFF.md (last-arc snapshot + exact next steps), and STATUS.html (single-writer).
  3. Commits and pushes — the arc is closed; any new chat on any surface orients from the three control files.
Memory

The Council remembers you — carefully. A private dossier corpus lives at ~/Documents/Claude/council/: dossier-core.md plus one annex per head (annex-eye.md, annex-ear.md, annex-hand.md).

Craft

The playbook

The Council rewards good petitions — and it was never only a software collective. Here is how to get the most from the full suite: the chains, the habits, the opening lines, worked examples step by step, and the uses beyond building.

Chained patterns

The full campaign — idea to shipped

Vet → Design → Build

EAR · gut-check→EAR · full council→EYE · full review→FORGE · work order→HANDOFF

Cheap kill first: a gut-check costs minutes. Survives? Full council stress-tests the premise. Still standing? The Eye designs it, Forge cuts the build order, and the arc closes with handoff. Each stage's output is the next stage's brief — carry the Ear's verdict into the Eye's intake, the winning direction into Forge's order.

The decision sprint

Gut-check early, full council rarely

The GUT-CHECK is deliberately cheap — use it the way you'd text a sharp friend: "is this stupid?" Save FULL COUNCIL for decisions with real stakes or real disagreement inside your own head. If the gut-check verdict surprises you, that's the signal to convene the full council.

The design ladder

LITE to feel, FULL to commit

"Quick ideas" gets you 3–4 hero mockups in minutes — enough to feel the territory. When one direction pulls at you, escalate: the FULL review pressure-tests it against five rivals and hands you build-ready assets for the winner. LITE explores; FULL decides.

Getting the most from every session

  • Give real context — point the Council at the actual repo, CLAUDE.md, or artifact. Judgments on real material beat judgments on descriptions of it.
  • Let them disagree — don't referee mid-debate or rescue your idea from Sentinel. The distillation at the end is where you re-enter.
  • Name the head when you know it — direct address skips a routing hop. Unsure? The front door costs one line.
  • Confirm dossier appends — the Council's read of you compounds across sessions, but only if you say yes to the proposed entries.
  • Mind the seams — software architecture belongs to Forge, UX architecture to Flow, visual direction to the cast. Asking the right owner gets the right answer.

Worked examples, step by step

Scenario · a life decision — no code anywhere near it

"Should I take the promotion?"

  1. Open cheap: "The Ear, gut-check: I've been offered the lead role on X. More money, more meetings, less building. Is taking it stupid?" — the Chief picks two or three seats; you get a verdict in minutes.
  2. Read the verdict for surprise. If it confirms your lean, you may be done. If it surprises you, that is the signal to escalate.
  3. Escalate with context: "Convene the full council on this" — and add what the gut-check exposed: your actual finances, the ten-year picture, what your family said. Real inputs, real debate.
  4. Re-enter at the distillation. The executive summary gives the verdict, where the seats split, and the thing you're avoiding — that last line is usually the answer.
  5. Consent to the dossier append. The Council's read of how you weigh money against meaning compounds into every future decision it helps you make.
Scenario · understanding a topic before an opinion forms

"Help me actually understand the hype around X"

  1. Petition the front door: "The council — I keep reading about X. Help me understand what's real." Garrick routes it to the Ear in study formation.
  2. Owl grounds it: what is actually established, the prior art, where the settled ground ends.
  3. Proof sifts the claims: which headline numbers are evidenced, which are marketing.
  4. Pulse reads the market: who is genuinely paying versus loudly posting.
  5. The Chief distils — a one-page brief: what's real, what's open, what to watch for. Ask Quill to turn it into a memo if it needs to travel.
Scenario · the full campaign, compressed

An idea becomes a shipped thing

  1. Gut-check the idea ("is this stupid?") — minutes, and most ideas rightly die here.
  2. Kickoff the survivor: worth-building check, one batched interview, scaffolded repo.
  3. Eye full review when the product has a face to design; pick the winner once.
  4. Forge cuts the work order carrying the Ear's verdict and the Eye's winning direction as its brief.
  5. Handoff closes the arc; the next chat, on any surface, orients in seconds.

Six good opening lines

  • Advice: "The Ear, gut-check: [decision] — here's my lean and my fear. Is it stupid?"
  • Debate a topic: "Convene the council: steelman both sides of [question] and tell me where the real crux is."
  • Critique writing: "The Ear, critique mode: here's my draft [essay/talk]. Does it clear the bar, and where's the weak joint?"
  • Evaluate a purchase or vendor: "The council: I'm choosing between [A] and [B] for [job]. Proof on the claims, Pulse on the market, Sentinel on what bites me in a year."
  • Red-team a plan: "Sentinel, pre-mortem this: it's a year out and [plan] is dead — what killed it, and what de-risks each cause?"
  • Design anything: "The Eye, quick ideas for [screen / room / deck] — three directions, just the feel."

Anti-patterns

Don't ask the Eye for software architecture

Stack, data models, and system design belong to Forge. The Eye owns how it looks, feels, and flows — including physical space — not how it's built.

Don't skip Kickoff for a new build

Going straight to Forge with a fresh idea skips the worth-building check and the batched interview — the two cheapest mistakes-avoided in the whole system.

Don't polish a premise the Ear hasn't seen

A full Eye review of an idea that dies in a five-minute gut-check is the most expensive way to learn it was stillborn.

Covenant

Keeping this true

This prospectus is the Council's always-synced readable surface — generated, never hand-drifted, and Steward owns the sync. Honesty about the mechanism: nothing monitors your chats in the background. Truth is kept by three layers, each catching what the one above it misses.

  1. Layer one — ambient memory (automatic, lossy). The platform's own memory absorbs new capabilities and rules from conversations on its own. Useful, but summarized and never authoritative — it is a lead, not a record.
  2. Layer two — the close-out ritual (deterministic). Any chat that births a capability, rule, or structural change ends with the Hand registering it before the chat closes: the annex gets the entry, CAPABILITIES.md gets the instrument, MANUAL.md §11 gets a one-line changelog entry (a separate commit — it's a different repo), and a regeneration of this guide is flagged for the same arc. The ritual, not the memory, is the guarantee.
  3. Layer three — the Steward Audit (the net). On a monthly cadence or on demand, Steward diffs everything: guide footer vs plugin.json, annex vs the guide's last-updated date, skills vs PERSONAS.md, memory vs the registry, and MANUAL.md vs the plugin version. Anything that slipped both layers above surfaces here as a flagged drift and dies in one regeneration arc.
  • Canonical sources — cast in PERSONAS.md, instruments in CAPABILITIES.md, method in each head's skills/*/SKILL.md. This page renders them; it never leads them.
  • Two published forms — the full-detail prospectus lives in the private plugin repo; a redacted twin (no local paths, no personal infrastructure) is published to the open web via the Press. Every regeneration updates both, and the redactions are re-applied every time.
  • Drift detection you can see — the footer below carries the plugin version and last-updated date. If it disagrees with plugin.json, this page has drifted: that is a defect, and a Steward fix.
