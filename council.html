kings-council v1.5.0

One King.
Twenty-one minds in service.

The King's Council is a standing court of elite specialists — designers, counsellors, and operators — convened on demand, built to disagree, and sworn to one outcome: the best possible version of your decision, your design, and your build.

Charter

The Concept

Most advice fails in one of two ways: a single voice with a single bias, or a crowd that politely agrees. The Council is engineered against both. It is a fixed cast of twenty-one named specialists organized under three heads — the Eye (design), the Ear (counsel), and the Hand (execution) — each member carrying a strong home philosophy, a declared bias, and the professional discipline to serve the work anyway.

The cast never rotates. The same designers author every review; the same councillors argue every debate; the same operators cut every work order. Familiarity is the feature: over time their voices become legible, their disagreements predictable in shape but not in outcome, and their accumulated read of you — kept in a private dossier system — steadily sharper.

The King's Eye

The full design house — visual, UX, product, and physical space. Six authors, one architect, one director. Beauty that works.

The King's Ear

Eight adversarial counsellors and a Chief who referees. They steelman first, clash second, and hand back a verdict — never a transcript.

The King's Hand

The execution arm. Decisions in, finished artifacts out — work orders, decks, runbooks. Never a status update in place of the thing.

What the whole court believes

Built to disagree

Every member's "optimizes for" is deliberately another's "will sacrifice." The friction is the product — parallel agreement is banned by charter.

Bias shapes how, never whether

Each specialist is opinionated by design but professional by oath: their philosophy shapes how they solve your problem, never whether they serve it.

Steelman before critique

No idea is attacked before its strongest honest version has been stated. Strawmen are beneath this court.

One front door

Every petition can enter through the Hand, who routes it in one line and returns one integrated deliverable — however many heads it took.

Memory with a firewall

The court keeps a dossier of how you think — read silently by the leads to sharpen judgment, never shown to the working cast, so their output stays honest.

Deliverables, not dialogue

Debates are distilled, reviews are scored, orders run end-to-end. You receive the finished thing and the reasoning that survived.

Provenance

How the Court Was Assembled

None of these seats is arbitrary. Each head was recruited against an explicit theory of cognitive diversity — the goal was never a team that gets along, but a team whose disagreements cover the whole territory of a problem.

The Ear — de Bono's hats, then beyond them

The counsel bench was recruited around Edward de Bono's Six Thinking Hats — the classic proof that a group reasons best when its members are assigned opposing modes rather than left to converge. Six seats map to the hats directly: Proof wears White (facts and data), Soul wears Red (feeling and intuition), Champion wears Yellow (optimism and value), Sentinel wears Black (caution and risk), Maker wears Green (creativity and alternatives), and Alistair wears Blue — the process hat, refereeing the rest.

Then the bench was extended, because the hats cover modes of thinking but not sources of knowledge: Premise interrogates the question itself before any hat goes on; Owl brings what the field already knows so the court never reasons from scratch; Pulse brings the one voice de Bono's room lacks entirely — the market's.

The Eye — a spectrum of opposed schools

The design cast was recruited in deliberate tension pairs from across the schools: rationalist structure (Grid) against emotional image-making (Vogue); brutal machine honesty (Steel) against calm human-centeredness (Calm); futurist spectacle (Neon) against platform tradition (Cupertino). One seat sits outside every school and owns whether any of it works (Flow), and the chair went to the tradition that refuses to separate function, craft, and the human at all — Danish Modern, in the person of Marlowe, who judges without authoring.

The Hand — the three verbs of delivery

Execution decomposes into exactly three verbs, and each got a seat: Forge builds, Quill writes, Steward runs — under a career chief of staff (Garrick) whose only craft is turning a decree into the finished thing.

The common thread: a fixed cast with declared biases. Rotation would make each session smarter and the court dumber — familiarity is what lets you calibrate a voice, and a declared bias is the only kind you can correct for.
Protocol

Convening the Council

Speak plainly and the right chamber opens. The skills trigger on natural language — "vet this idea," "design this," "spec the build" — or by direct address. All five skills reach every surface (Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cowork, claude.ai web and mobile); the /kings-council:handoff command runs in Claude Code and Cowork.

First Head · Design

The King's Eye

The court's full design house — a fixed cast of six authoring designers, one interaction architect who audits them all, and a Creative Director who judges, scores, and presents. Its remit spans visual design, UX, product, and physical/building architecture.

What the Eye delivers

  • FULL review — six genuinely divergent directions, each anchored to a named design movement (no two share one), rendered as live interactive mock UI across every primary flow, scored on a project-weighted rubric, with a winner, runner-up, and optional hybrid.
  • Build-ready handoff — after you confirm the winner: compilable design tokens (Swift or CSS, stack-detected), exhaustive design-system specs for the top two, a paste-ready design-system prompt for any AI coding tool, and RECOMMENDATION.md.
  • LITE ideation — 3–4 fast hero-screen mockups to feel out territory before committing. No scoring, no assets, genuinely quick.
  • The Flow Review — every direction, full or lite, is independently audited for UX soundness — flows, states, error paths, accessibility — before it reaches you. Nothing beautiful-but-broken ships.
  • Physical space — rooms, structures, environments. The Eye's remit covers built space, not just screens.
How it runs: Marlowe orients on your actual project, holds one short intake meeting for your steer, briefs the cast (dossier firewalled), the six author, Flow audits, Marlowe scores and recommends — then pauses exactly once for your winner pick before cutting handoff assets. Say "quick ideas" for LITE; anything else runs FULL.

Second Head · Counsel

The King's Ear

Eight expert thinking-partners and a Chief who referees. Not a panel of nodding heads — a structured adversarial court that steelmans your idea, tries honestly to break it, and returns a verdict you can act on. Any domain: ventures, software, intellectual and theological questions, life decisions.

What the Ear delivers

  • GUT-CHECK — the Chief plus the two or three most relevant seats, a fast sharp take, a 2–4 sentence verdict. The recommended default for quick asks: "is this stupid?"
  • FULL COUNCIL — all eight: steelman first, then individual challenges, then genuine cross-examination refereed by the Chief — ending in an executive summary, never a transcript.
  • CRITIQUE — full cast, evaluative frame: does this built thing or formed idea clear the bar? What's missing? What would a user, reader, or critic say?
  • The distillation — every session ends with the verdict, the 3–5 points that matter ranked, where the council split, the load-bearing critique for how you think — and the thing you're avoiding.
House rules: steelman before critique — no strawmen. Councillors cross-examine each other, not just you, and concede points they lose. Each seat's pre-mortems, evidence demands, and reframes adapt to the domain — a theology paper gets a doctrinal pre-mortem, a life decision a regret pre-mortem.

Third Head · Execution

The King's Hand

The execution arm. Decisions go in; finished artifacts come out. Sir Garrick Vale receives the decree, decomposes it, assigns the right seat or seats, and returns the finished thing — never a status update in place of a deliverable.

What the Hand delivers

  • Forge — Claude Code work orders, technical specs, tech-stack selection, data models, system and functional diagrams. Software architecture lives here — not with the Eye.
  • Quill — PM and stakeholder deliverables: status reports, decks, memos, comms — decision-first, audience-calibrated.
  • Steward — environment maintenance runbooks, config discipline, migration prep, the handoff system, the environment manual — and this guide's sync.
  • Kickoff routing — a brand-new software project never goes straight to Forge: Garrick routes it to the Kickoff rite first, so the worth-building check and batched interview happen before any scaffold.
What the Hand is not: a debating chamber. If your petition needs vetting, Garrick routes it to the Ear first; if it needs designing, to the Eye — then the Hand builds what survived.

The Toolbelt

The Hand's Instruments

The cast is fixed; the toolbelt grows. Every new capability the court gains joins as an instrument — a named, owner-assigned, deterministic procedure — never as a new persona. Instruments are registered in CAPABILITIES.md at the repo root (the canonical registry) with a one-line index in the Hand's skill, which is what makes them invocable from every surface.

Owner: Quill · trigger — "press this" / "send to the press"

Press

Publishes a single self-contained HTML page to the open web in one call. Procedure: redact personal infrastructure if the source is private → one GitHub MCP call to a public GitHub Pages repo (live within seconds, one-word lowercase filename) → verify byte-exact by comparing local git hash-object against the returned blob SHA. Recovery on a wedged MCP: full Claude Desktop relaunch, then push — never reroute to a relay.

Owner: Steward · trigger — "run the audit" / monthly cadence

Steward Audit

The drift detector. Checks: guide footer version vs plugin.json; annex entries newer than the guide's last-updated date; skills vs PERSONAS.md; capabilities in recent memory that never reached CAPABILITIES.md. Output: a severity-flagged drift report and, where drift exists, a single regeneration arc.

Owner: Garrick + Steward · trigger — "optimization review" / quarterly

Optimization Review

Audits the King's own configuration: saved memory, global preferences, and per-project instructions — hunting contradictions, stale rules, and project-local rules that deserve promotion to global (or the reverse). Delivers ready-to-paste diffs; memory edits are proposed and applied only on your confirmation.

Adding an instrument — the standing process

  1. Name it — one word — and assign an owner seat. If no seat fits, the remit of an existing seat is widened; the cast does not grow.
  2. Define it — trigger phrase, deterministic procedure, tools used, verification step, failure recovery.
  3. Register it — entry in CAPABILITIES.md, one-line index in the Hand's skill, a card in this section, and an annex note if it touches a pipeline. The Hand performs the registration in the same chat that birthed the capability, before close-out.
Ceremony

The Two Rites

Two ceremonies bracket every project's life: Kickoff opens it, and Handoff closes each arc of work. Between them, the council's memory system keeps every surface oriented.

Kickoff — idea to handoff-ready repo

Trigger with "new project," "kickoff," "I have an idea for an app/tool." Runs anywhere; scaffolds only in Claude Code.

  1. Worth-building check — one honest pass: names tools that already solve it, offers a King's Ear gut-check if the verdict is murky.
  2. One batched interview — purpose, users, platform, stack, data sensitivity, name, git remote, v0.1 success. Every question up front; zero drip-feeding after.
  3. Scaffold (Claude Code only) — repo under the local projects root, git initialized, control files seeded with the real answers.
  4. First arc-close — runs /kings-council:handoff, emits a paste-ready Claude Desktop project block, registers the project in the environment manual.
On any other surface the rite returns a single deterministic Claude Code work order instead — nothing half-scaffolded, ever.

Handoff — closing an arc

The command: /kings-council:handoff (Claude Code and Cowork).

  1. Consumes ORCHESTRATION.md — Desktop's append-only inbox of decisions, work-order summaries, and flagged RULE: entries.
  2. Updates CLAUDE.md (durable truth), HANDOFF.md (last-arc snapshot + exact next steps), and STATUS.html (single-writer).
  3. Commits and pushes — the arc is closed; any new chat on any surface orients from the three control files.
Memory

The Dossier System

The council remembers you — carefully. A private dossier corpus lives locally, outside every repo: a shared core file plus one annex per head.

  • Read silently — the leads (Marlowe, Alistair, Garrick) consult it at the start of a session to sharpen judgment. No ceremony, no announcement.
  • The firewall — the working cast never sees it. Grid doesn't design toward your known tastes; Champion doesn't steelman toward your known hopes. Their output stays honest; the leads' judgment is what the dossier sharpens.
  • Propose, then confirm — new entries are proposed openly at session end and appended only with your consent. Append-only; earlier entries are never rewritten.
  • Off GitHub — the corpus is deliberately not in any repo. In machine migrations, it is hand-carried.
Craft

Working with the Council

The council rewards good petitions — and it was never only a software court. Here is how to get the most from the full suite: the chains, the habits, the opening lines, worked examples step by step, and the uses beyond building.

Chained patterns

The full campaign — idea to shipped

Vet → Design → Build

EAR · gut-check→EAR · full council→EYE · full review→FORGE · work order→HANDOFF

Cheap kill first: a gut-check costs minutes. Survives? Full council stress-tests the premise. Still standing? The Eye designs it, Forge cuts the build order, and the arc closes with handoff. Each stage's output is the next stage's brief — carry the Ear's verdict into the Eye's intake, the winning direction into Forge's order.

The decision sprint

Gut-check early, full council rarely

The GUT-CHECK is deliberately cheap — use it the way you'd text a sharp friend: "is this stupid?" Save FULL COUNCIL for decisions with real stakes or real disagreement inside your own head. If the gut-check verdict surprises you, that's the signal to convene the full court.

The design ladder

LITE to feel, FULL to commit

"Quick ideas" gets you 3–4 hero mockups in minutes — enough to feel the territory. When one direction pulls at you, escalate: the FULL review pressure-tests it against five rivals and hands you build-ready assets for the winner. LITE explores; FULL decides.

Getting the most from every session

  • Give real context — point the council at the actual repo, CLAUDE.md, or artifact. Judgments on real material beat judgments on descriptions of it.
  • Let them disagree — don't referee mid-debate or rescue your idea from Sentinel. The distillation at the end is where you re-enter.
  • Name the head when you know it — direct address skips a routing hop. Unsure? The front door costs one line.
  • Confirm dossier appends — the council's read of you compounds across sessions, but only if you say yes to the proposed entries.
  • Mind the seams — software architecture belongs to Forge, UX architecture to Flow, visual direction to the cast. Asking the right owner gets the right answer.

Worked examples, step by step

Scenario · a life decision — no code anywhere near it

"Should I take the promotion?"

  1. Open cheap: "Kings ear, gut-check: I've been offered the lead role on X. More money, more meetings, less building. Is taking it stupid?" — the Chief picks two or three seats; you get a verdict in minutes.
  2. Read the verdict for surprise. If it confirms your lean, you may be done. If it surprises you, that is the signal to escalate.
  3. Escalate with context: "Convene the full council on this" — and add what the gut-check exposed: your actual finances, the ten-year picture, what your family said. Real inputs, real debate.
  4. Re-enter at the distillation. The executive summary gives the verdict, where the seats split, and the thing you're avoiding — that last line is usually the answer.
  5. Consent to the dossier append. The court's read of how you weigh money against meaning compounds into every future decision it helps you make.
Scenario · understanding a topic before an opinion forms

"Help me actually understand the hype around X"

  1. Petition the front door: "Kings council — I keep reading about X. Help me understand what's real." Garrick routes it to the Ear in study formation.
  2. Owl grounds it: what is actually established, the prior art, where the settled ground ends.
  3. Proof sifts the claims: which headline numbers are evidenced, which are marketing.
  4. Pulse reads the market: who is genuinely paying versus loudly posting.
  5. The Chief distils — a one-page brief: what's real, what's open, what to watch for. Ask Quill to turn it into a memo if it needs to travel.
Scenario · the full campaign, compressed

An idea becomes a shipped thing

  1. Gut-check the idea ("is this stupid?") — minutes, and most ideas rightly die here.
  2. Kickoff the survivor: worth-building check, one batched interview, scaffolded repo.
  3. Eye full review when the product has a face to design; pick the winner once.
  4. Forge cuts the work order carrying the Ear's verdict and the Eye's winning direction as its brief.
  5. Handoff closes the arc; the next chat, on any surface, orients in seconds.

Six good opening lines

  • Advice: "Kings ear, gut-check: [decision] — here's my lean and my fear. Is it stupid?"
  • Debate a topic: "Convene the council: steelman both sides of [question] and tell me where the real crux is."
  • Critique writing: "Kings ear, critique mode: here's my draft [essay/talk]. Does it clear the bar, and where's the weak joint?"
  • Evaluate a purchase or vendor: "Kings council: I'm choosing between [A] and [B] for [job]. Proof on the claims, Pulse on the market, Sentinel on what bites me in a year."
  • Red-team a plan: "Sentinel, pre-mortem this: it's a year out and [plan] is dead — what killed it, and what de-risks each cause?"
  • Design anything: "Kings eye, quick ideas for [screen / room / deck] — three directions, just the feel."

Beyond software — uses you may not have considered

Ear · full council or gut-check

Life & career decisions

Role changes, big purchases, relocation, a new workstation build. Sentinel runs a regret pre-mortem, Soul weighs what it means, Proof demands the actual numbers, Premise asks whether you're even deciding the right question.

Quill + Sentinel + Premise

Stakeholder work at the day job

Quill drafts the steering-committee deck or workstream status that ends the meeting early. Before a go-live: Sentinel pre-mortems the cutover. Before a metrics review: Premise interrogates whether the KPI measures the goal.

Eye · full or lite

Physical space

The Eye's remit includes built space — a home-office redesign, a workshop layout, landscaping. Six directions on a room work exactly like six directions on a screen.

Ear · critique mode

Writing, talks, and arguments

Hand the council a draft essay, a presentation, or a position you intend to defend. CRITIQUE evaluates it against what good looks like; the cross-examination finds the weak joint before your audience does.

Owl, with Proof on call

Learning a field fast

"What is already known about X?" is Owl's entire purpose — prior art, established frameworks, where the settled ground ends and the open questions begin. Proof flags which claims in the field are actually well-evidenced.

Sentinel + Champion + Pulse

Negotiation & vendor evaluation

Champion steelmans the other side's position, Sentinel maps your exposure, Pulse reads what the counterparty's market actually rewards. Works for salary talks, vendor selection, and contract renewals alike.

Steward

Environment & migration work

Diagnostics, config hygiene, the machine-migration runbook — read-only first, one change at a time, everything designed to carry over cleanly.

Anti-patterns

Don't ask the Eye for software architecture

Stack, data models, and system design belong to Forge. The Eye owns how it looks, feels, and flows — including physical space — not how it's built.

Don't skip Kickoff for a new build

Going straight to Forge with a fresh idea skips the worth-building check and the batched interview — the two cheapest mistakes-avoided in the whole system.

Don't polish a premise the Ear hasn't seen

A full Eye review of an idea that dies in a five-minute gut-check is the most expensive way to learn it was stillborn.

Covenant

Keeping This Guide True

This guide is the council's always-synced readable surface — generated, never hand-drifted, and Steward owns the sync. Honesty about the mechanism: nothing monitors your chats in the background. Truth is kept by three layers, each catching what the one above it misses.

  1. Layer one — ambient memory (automatic, lossy). The platform's own memory absorbs new capabilities and rules from conversations on its own. Useful, but summarized and never authoritative — it is a lead, not a record.
  2. Layer two — the close-out ritual (deterministic). Any chat that births a capability, rule, or structural change ends with the Hand registering it before the chat closes: the annex gets the entry, CAPABILITIES.md gets the instrument, and a regeneration of this guide is flagged for the same arc. The ritual, not the memory, is the guarantee.
  3. Layer three — the Steward Audit (the net). On a monthly cadence or on demand, Steward diffs everything: guide footer vs plugin.json, annex vs the guide's last-updated date, skills vs PERSONAS.md, memory vs the registry. Anything that slipped both layers above surfaces here as a flagged drift and dies in one regeneration arc.
  • Canonical sources — cast in PERSONAS.md, instruments in CAPABILITIES.md, method in each head's skills/*/SKILL.md. This page renders them; it never leads them.
  • Two published forms — the full-detail guide lives in the private plugin repo; a redacted twin (no local paths, no personal infrastructure) is published to the open web via the Press. Every regeneration updates both, and the redactions are re-applied every time.
  • Drift detection you can see — the footer below carries the plugin version and last-updated date. If it disagrees with plugin.json, this page has drifted: that is a defect, and a Steward fix.
